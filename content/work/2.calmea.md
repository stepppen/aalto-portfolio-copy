::cs-section{n="02" title="The app: a night of sleep, readable"}
The app turns the night's signals into a few things you can read at a glance: a quality score, how long and how regularly you slept, and recommendations in plain words. Day, week and month views show the same night in context.

The same app runs the bed. You switch it on, set intensity and vibration, start a meditation programme and set your bed time.

::cs-section{n="03" title="Where the data lives"}
Calmea handles health data, and Swiss law (the revised Federal Act on Data Protection) only lets it leave the country for places with adequate protection. So before building, I mapped what the system has to do: the bed's ESP32 sends sleep data every few hours, status updates and error reports, and the app switches the bed on and off, changes the rocking mode and updates settings.

Then I compared three architectures. The cheapest builds fast on EU hosting and leaves compliance for later. The middle one moves the backend to Swiss hosting and works towards certification. The last keeps everything in Switzerland, which is the strongest case for approval and for marketing, but costs the most. Each one weighed cost against how likely Swissmedic would be to approve it.

:::cs-media{src="/Calmea/architecture.webp" alt="Three hosting architectures for Calmea side by side, from fast and EU-hosted to fully Swiss, with costs, advantages and disadvantages." caption="Three options, from fast and cheap to 100% Swiss, with costs and trade-offs."}
