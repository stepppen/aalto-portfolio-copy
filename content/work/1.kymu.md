::cs-section{n="01" title="Where it started"}
Kymu began with children. For the thesis we started from the evidence on paediatric neuromuscular therapy. Literature reviews and expert interviews kept pointing at the same problems: low adherence to home exercises, little data to personalise therapy, and a heavy logistical load on families.

:::cs-media{src="/Kymu/overview.webp" alt="Research overview: target groups, synthesised core problems and project goals." caption="The thesis research: target groups, the main problems, and the goals we set from them."}

::cs-section{n="02" title="From children to adults"}
The problems we found weren't specific to children. Adults in orthopaedic and geriatric rehabilitation have the same gap between the clinic and home, and there are far more of them: over 50,000 patients a year in Switzerland, against fewer than 1,000 with rare muscular diseases. That's a 50 times larger market and a faster regulatory path, so we widened Kymu to adults.

The mission stayed the same: rehabilitation that is motivating and accessible in the long term.
::

::cs-section{n="03" title="Any TV, one phone"}
Kymu needs no special hardware. A smartphone, tablet or PC pairs with a normal TV or monitor. The phone's camera estimates the pose in real time and turns movement straight into gameplay. The same setup works on a ward and in a living room.

:::cs-media{src="/Kymu/phone-tv.webp" alt="Side by side: a phone camera view of a woman squatting, and the TV showing the game it controls." caption="The phone tracks the movement, the TV shows the game."}
:::

Two ideas make the games work for therapy. The movement is decoupled from the game, so one game can serve many different exercise protocols. And the difficulty adapts in real time to each patient's limits.
::

::cs-section{n="05" title="The therapist platform"}
Therapists stay in charge. Kymu's motion engine tracks more than 30 clinical protocols, from a single joint's range of motion to functional movements. In the platform, therapists check how an exercise was done and catch compensation movements, adjust each patient's parameters, and build training plans.

:::cs-grid{cols="2"}
::::cs-media{src="/Kymu/studio-gallery.webp" alt="A gallery of exercise figures in the Kymu therapist platform, from shoulder rotations to squats." caption="The exercise library."}

::cs-section{n="06" title="From the clinic to home"}
Kymu is built as continuous care. It starts in person and ends with the patient training on their own, long after the regular sessions run out.

:::cs-diagram
---

  - title: Setup
    detail: The therapist sets up, instructs and calibrates, in person.
  - title: Guided training
    detail: The patient trains with a plan, in the clinic or at home. The therapist can supervise.
  - title: Review
    detail: The therapist analyses the data and adjusts the therapy.
  - title: On their own
    detail: Self-supervised, long-term rehabilitation, in the clinic or at home.

::cs-section{n="07" title="Testing with people"}
We work in short sprints, each ending with expert interviews and user tests that decide what changes next. More than 20 people have tested the system so far, from patients and healthy adults to physiotherapists. The pilot at Lucerne Cantonal Hospital is meant to show three things: how Kymu fits everyday inpatient physiotherapy, who would pay for it, and first signs of its impact.
::
